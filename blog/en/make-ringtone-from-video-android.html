How to make a ringtone from a video on Android

12 September 2026FightTech7 min read

The chorus of a concert clip, a line from a family video, the jingle at the end of a short film: if the sound is in a video on your phone, it can be your ringtone. This guide walks through it in Video to MP3, with the lengths, file sizes and settings that actually matter for a phone speaker.

Short answer

Open Video to MP3, tap MP3 next to the video, drag the two Trim handles around 20–30 seconds, keep MP3 at 128 or 192 kbps and tap Start. On the result screen tap Ringtone and choose Phone ringtone. Android asks once for the “Modify system settings” permission. None of these steps cost credits.

1. Before you start

  • An Android phone on Android 8.0 or newer with Video to MP3 installed from Google Play.
  • The video saved on the phone. The Convert tab lists the videos in your phone's storage; the app works with files you already have and does not download anything.
  • A clip you have the right to use. A ringtone for your own phone is personal use, but the sound still belongs to whoever made it.

The conversion runs on your device, so the video is never uploaded and it stays exactly as it was. The ringtone is a new audio file saved next to your other music.

2. How long a ringtone should be

Android loops a ringtone for as long as the phone rings. Many carriers send an unanswered call to voicemail after roughly 20 to 30 seconds, so a clip of that length plays once, start to finish, without restarting mid-phrase. Notifications are different: you hear them for a moment, so a short sound works best. Alarms loop until you dismiss them, so they can run longer.

UseGood lengthWhy
Phone ringtone20–30 sCovers a typical ring before voicemail without looping
Notification sound1–3 sA long notification cuts into whatever you are listening to
Alarm sound30–60 sLoops until dismissed; a longer clip repeats less often

3. Make the ringtone in 8 steps

Everything happens on one screen, Extract audio, which opens when you tap MP3 next to a video. The labels below are the ones you will see in the app.

Plan on a couple of minutes the first time, most of it spent finding the exact moment you want.

  1. Open Video to MP3. On the Convert tab, find the video and tap MP3 on its row.
  2. Under Trim, drag the two handles around the part you want. The waveform shows loud and quiet passages, so you can see where a chorus starts.
  3. For exact times, tap the time range and type the start and end in mm:ss, for example 1:05 to 1:32. Decimals such as 1:05.5 work too.
  4. Tap Play the selected part and listen. Nudge the handles until the clip starts on a beat or on the first word.
  5. Optionally tap Trim silence to drop quiet air at the start and end of the selection, so the ring starts the moment a call arrives.
  6. In Output name, type a name you will recognise in Android's sound list, such as “Chorus ringtone”.
  7. Leave Format on MP3 and pick Bitrate 128 or 192 kbps, then tap Start. The app confirms “Done — saved to Music/Video to Audio”.
  8. Tap Ringtone, then Phone ringtone. The first time, Android opens the Modify system settings page: switch it on for the app, go back, and the ringtone is set.

The file stays in the app afterwards: the Music tab lists every file the app has made, so you can set it again later or use it as a notification instead.

4. Format and bitrate: what a phone speaker needs

A ringtone plays through a small speaker, often in a pocket. The difference between 192 and 320 kbps that you might hear on good headphones disappears there, and even 128 kbps sounds clean. File size hardly matters either: every lossy option below stays under about a megabyte and a quarter for 30 seconds.

The sums are simple: bitrate × seconds ÷ 8. At 128 kbps that is 128,000 × 30 ÷ 8 = 480,000 bytes.

MP3, M4A or M4R? Android accepts MP3 and M4A (AAC) as ringtones, and MP3 is the safest choice across phones. M4R is the same AAC audio as M4A with a different file extension, which some other phones expect for ringtones; on Android you do not need it.

5. A clean start and a clean loop

The most common complaint about a homemade ringtone is the loop: the clip ends mid-note, then jumps straight back to the start. Three settings on the same screen fix that.

  • Cut at a pause. Flat parts of the waveform are quiet. Ending the selection there, or on the last beat of a bar, makes the restart sound deliberate.
  • Fade in and Fade out under Effects offer 1, 2, 3 or 5 seconds. A 1–2 second fade-out softens the loop. Skip the fade-in on a ringtone, since you want to hear it at once; for an alarm, a 3–5 second fade-in wakes you more gently.
  • Volume runs from 25% to 300%, with quick picks at 100, 150, 200 and 300%. Speech from a phone video is often quiet, so 150% helps. Above 200% the app warns that the sound may clip and distort, so preview before you save.

Trim, Trim silence and Volume are free. On the free plan, effects such as fades, Normalize loudness or Reduce noise cost 1 credit per output file. You start with 5 credits and can earn 1 for each rewarded ad, up to 5 a day; Premium removes credit costs and ads.

6. Ringtone, notification or alarm

The Ringtone button on the result screen opens Set as… with four choices:

  • Phone ringtone — incoming calls.
  • Notification sound — messages and app alerts.
  • Alarm sound — the default alarm.
  • Ringtone for a contact — one person gets their own ring. This one is Premium and asks for access to your contacts.

Made the file earlier? Open the Music tab, long-press the file and choose Set as ringtone. The same option is in Recent files.

7. Changing it back

Tired of it, or set the wrong clip as your alarm? Open the app's Settings and tap Restore original ringtone. It puts back the ringtone, notification and alarm sounds the app replaced. You can also pick any other sound in Android's own sound settings; the audio file stays in Music/Video to Audio until you delete it.

Frequently asked questions

Is making a ringtone free?

Yes. Trimming, saving as MP3 or M4A at up to 192 kbps, and setting a phone ringtone, notification or alarm sound cost nothing. On the free plan, fades and other effects, and 256 or 320 kbps, cost 1 credit per file. A ringtone for a single contact is Premium.

Why does Android ask to “Modify system settings”?

Android only lets an app change the default ringtone, notification or alarm sound after you allow that permission. You grant it once. Settings → Restore original ringtone puts the previous sounds back.

How long should a ringtone be?

About 20 to 30 seconds for calls, 1 to 3 seconds for notifications and 30 to 60 seconds for an alarm.

Should I use MP3 or M4A?

Both work on Android. MP3 at 128 or 192 kbps is the safe choice; 320 kbps adds nothing you can hear through a phone speaker.

Is my video uploaded or changed?

No. The conversion runs on your device, and the original video stays exactly as it was.
